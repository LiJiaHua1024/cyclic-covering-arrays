# Chapter 3: Finite Cases: Exact Integrality Gaps for $n \in \{13, 14, 15\}$

Across the three consecutive cycle lengths $n \in \{13, 14, 15\}$, an invariant exact structure emerges:
$$\boxed{LP(n) = 12, \qquad N(n) = 13, \qquad \text{Integrality Gap } G(n) = 1.}$$

---

## 1. Cycle $C_{13}$ (Odd, No Alternating Independent Set)

On $C_{13}$, the tight condition $2a + 3b = 13$ has integer solutions $(a, b) \in \{(5, 1), (2, 3)\}$.
- Class $(5, 1)$: 1 orbit of 13 rows, generated by mask $A = 1365$ (`1010101010100`).
- Class $(2, 3)$: 2 orbits of 13 rows each:
  - $B = 1173$ (the two 2-gaps are consecutive);
  - $C = 1189$ (the two 2-gaps are non-consecutive).
Total tight candidates: exactly **39 rows**.

Any putative 12-row integer solution must select $(p, q, r) = (5, 6, 1)$ rows from orbits $A, B, C$.
Normalizing the unique $C$-row to $1189$, an exact integer linear combination $W(m)$ with coefficients:
$$\alpha = (-6, 5, 5, 5, -6, -6, -6, 5, 5, 5, 5, -6, -6)$$
$$\beta = (-4, 7, 7, -4, -4, -4, -4, 7, 7, 7, -4, -4, -4)$$
yields:
- $W(\text{rot}_j(B)) = 0$ for all $j$.
- $W(\text{rot}_j(A)) = 11$ if $j=5$ (mask $2725$), and $0$ otherwise.
- $W(C) = 15$.

Summing over the 12 rows requires:
$$\sum W(x) = 2 \sum (\alpha_i + \beta_i) = 4$$
However, $15 + 11 z_{2725} = 4 \implies z_{2725} = -1$, contradicting the non-negativity of row counts.
**Remarkably, this impossibility holds even if test rows may be repeated.**

An explicit 13-row solution is given in `data/solution_c13.txt`.
The rational LP witness assigns weights $5/13, 6/13, 1/13$ to orbits $A, B, C$, giving exact objective $12$.
Hence $LP(13) = 12, N(13) = 13$.

## 2. Cycle $C_{14}$ (Even, With Alternating Independent Sets)

On $C_{14}$, the tight condition yields 37 equality rows partitioned into 4 cyclic orbits (including the two alternating maximum independent sets).
The impossibility of 12 distinct rows is certified by:
1. A 19-term integer certificate $Q(x)$ with $\sum Q(x) = -2$ for 12 rows.
2. Modulo 7 analysis: $Q(x) \equiv 1 \pmod 7$ on the 2 alternating rows and $0 \pmod 7$ on all others.
   Because at most 2 alternating rows can be chosen without duplicates, $\sum Q \equiv h \in \{0, 1, 2\} \pmod 7$, contradicting $-2 \equiv 5 \pmod 7$.
3. Exact subset-sum check confirms no subset of distinct equality rows can sum to $-2$.

An explicit 13-row solution is given in `data/solution_c14.txt`.
A symmetric 37-row rational witness confirms $LP(14) = 12$.

## 3. Cycle $C_{15}$ (Odd, Divisible by 3)

On $C_{15}$, tight rows correspond to $2a + 3b = 15 \implies (a, b) \in \{(6, 1), (3, 3), (0, 5)\}$.
The $(0, 5)$ class corresponds to the 3 rotating independent sets consisting exclusively of 3-gaps (`100100100100100`).
The infeasibility of 12 rows is established via a finite field rank certificate.
Matching upper bound $N(15) = 13$ and fractional witness $LP(15) = 12$.
